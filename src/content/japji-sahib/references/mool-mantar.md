### Jarnail Singh — "Understanding Jap" (Sikhspectrum.com) — commentary on the Mool Mantar

This essay treats the Mool Mantar as the "abstract" of Sikh theology, placed at the head of Guru Granth Sahib and (in full or abridged form) at the start of each of the 31 Raga anthologies — by the essay's own count, occurring 568 times across the text. Key points, attributed to Jarnail Singh's essay:

- Argues the terms "Beej Mantar"/"Mool Mantar" (seed-teaching / root-teaching), while used elsewhere in Gurbani for the Guru's teaching generally, are never applied by the Gurus to this specific verse — and that calling it that shouldn't be mistaken for Sikhism endorsing Hindu-style mantra-chanting for magical effect. "Mantar" in Gurbani means teaching (updesh), not an incantation.
- On "Ik Oankar": argues Guru Nanak deliberately used the numeral **1** (Ik), not the word, to lock the meaning to "one and only" and rule out the ambiguous senses of the word "ik" (first/sole/unique) and of "Oan" (which elsewhere can mean an opening Hindu invocation, or "yes sir"). The suffix "Kar" conveys something continuously pervading and happening — so Ikoankar is read as "the One God who pervades and happens everywhere."
- On "Sat Nam": Sat = everlasting isness/existence, Nam = name/fame/glory — unlike human names (which don't represent who we are), the names given to God do represent real qualities witnessed by people ("Kirtam", artificial names), but no single name is *the* real name; God's own glory pre-existed and will outlive the world (cites the Pauri 1 close, "Aad sach jugaad sach...", and Pauri 27's "hai bhee hosee jaa-ay na jaasee rachnaa jin rachaa-ee").
- On "Karta Purakh": Karta = the sole Creator (matter cannot be created by humans, only reshaped — cites Pauri 31, "asan loey loey bhandaar"). Purakh here is read as *not* meaning "male person" (its ordinary sense) but the Supreme Being pervading both inside and outside creation; the essay explicitly contrasts this with the Rig Vedic cosmic being "Purush" (with ten thousand heads/eyes/feet), which it argues this term rejects.
- On "Nirbhau Nirvair": chosen, the essay argues, over more generic epithets like "omnipotent/omniscient"; Nirbhau (fearless) and Nirvair (without enmity) are read as mutually defining — one can't be fearless while harboring enmity, or free of enmity while afraid.
- On "Akal Murat": Akal = beyond death/time; Murat = form/image — grammatically feminine in Punjabi, which the essay reads as evidence Gurbani doesn't assign God a fixed gender (God is called both mother and father elsewhere). Pairing an intangible adjective (Akal) with a tangible noun (Murat) is read as a rejection of idol-worship without denying God's reality.
- On "Ajooni Saibhang": Ajooni = unborn/not incarnated; Saibhang = self-existent — together read as an outright rejection of avatar/incarnation doctrine, citing a strong Guru Arjan verse (p.1136) that says the tongue claiming God took birth as Krishna deserves to be burnt.
- On "Gur Prasad": Gur = the knowledge that leads from darkness to light; Prasad = grace/blessing — we can work at gaining this knowledge through the Guru's teaching, but cannot force the outcome, "like a student who works hard on his studies but can not be an examiner for his own exam."
- Concludes each word of the Mool Mantar interlocks with its neighbors like "blocks of meaning," and that it should be read as a compact, logically constructed thesis statement of Sikh theology — not recited as an incantation.

ਸਰੀਰ ਨਾਸ-ਰਹਿਤ ਹੈ), ਜੋ ਜੂਨਾਂ ਵਿਚ ਨਹੀਂ ਆਉਂਦਾ, ਜਿਸ ਦਾ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਸ਼ ਆਪਣੇ ਆਪ ਤੋਂ ਹੋਇਆ ਹੈ ਅਤੇ
ਜੋ ਸਤਿਗੁਰੂ ਦੀ ਕਿਰਪਾ ਨਾਲ ਮਿਲਦਾ ਹੈ।
ਨੋਟ-ਇਹ ਉਪਰੋਕਤ ਗੁਰਸਿੱਖੀ ਦਾ ਮੂਲ-ਮੰਤਰ ਹੈ । ਇਸ ਤੋਂ ਅਗਾਂਹ ਲਿਖੀ ਗਈ ਬਾਣੀ ਦਾ ਨਾਮ ਹੈ 'ਜਪੁ'
। ਇਹ ਗੱਲ ਚੇਤੇ ਰੱਖਣ ਵਾਲੀ ਹੈ ਕਿ ਇਹ ਮੂਲ-ਮੰਤਰ ਵੱਖਰੀ ਚੀਜ਼ ਹੈ ਤੇ ਬਾਣੀ 'ਜਪੁ' ਵੱਖਰੀ ਹੈ । ਸ੍ਰੀ
ਗੁਰੂ ਗ੍ਰੰਥ ਸਾਹਿਬ ਦੇ ਸ਼ੁਰੂ ਵਿਚ ਇਹ ਮੂਲ-ਮੰਤਰ ਲਿਖਿਆ ਹੈ, ਜਿਵੇਂ ਹਰੇਕ ਰਾਗ ਦੇ ਸ਼ੁਰੂ ਵਿਚ ਭੀ ਲਿਖਿਆ
ਮਿਲਦਾ ਹੈ । ਬਾਣੀ 'ਜਪੁ' ਲਫ਼ਜ਼ 'ਆਦਿ ਸਚੁ' ਤੋਂ ਸ਼ੁਰੂ ਹੁੰਦੀ ਹੈ । ਆਸਾ ਦੀ ਵਾਰ ਦੇ ਸ਼ੁਰੂ ਵਿਚ ਭੀ
 ਇਹੀ ਮੂਲ-ਮੰਤਰ ਹੈ, ਪਰ 'ਵਾਰ' ਨਾਲ ਇਸ ਦਾ ਕੋਈ ਸੰਬੰਧ ਨਹੀਂ ਹੈ, ਤਿਵੇਂ ਹੀ ਇੱਥੇ ਹੈ । 'ਜਪੁ' ਦੇ
ਅਰੰਭ ਵਿਚ ਮੰਗਲਾਚਰਨ ਦੇ ਤੌਰ ਤੇ ਇਕ ਸਲੋਕ ਉਚਾਰਿਆ ਗਿਆ ਹੈ । ਫਿਰ ‘ਜਪੁ’ ਸਾਹਿਬ ਦੀਆਂ ੩੮
ਪਉੜੀਆਂ ਹਨ।

 ॥ਜਪੁ॥
ਇਸ ਸਾਰੀ ਬਾਣੀ ਦਾ ਨਾਮ 'ਜਪੁ' ਹੈ ।

 ਆਦਿ ਸਚੁ ਜੁਗਾਦਿ ਸਚੁ ॥ ਹੈ ਭੀ ਸਚੁ
 ਨਾਨਕ ਹੋਸੀ ਭੀ ਸਚੁ ॥ ੧ ॥

ਪਦ ਅਰਥ — ਆਦਿ-ਮੁੱਢ ਤੋਂ । ਸਚੁ-ਹੋਂਦ ਵਾਲਾ । ਸ਼ਬਦ 'ਸਚੁ' ਸੰਸਕ੍ਰਿਤ ਦੇ 'ਸਤਯ' ਦਾ ਪ੍ਰਾਕ੍ਰਿਤ ਹੈ,
ਜਿਸ ਦਾ ਧਾਤੂ 'ਅਸ' ਹੈ । 'ਅਸ' ਦਾ ਅਰਥ ਹੈ 'ਹੋਣਾ' । ਜੁਗਾਦਿ-ਜੁਗਾਂ ਦੇ ਮੁੱਢ ਤੋਂ । ਹੈ-ਭਾਵ, ਇਸ
ਵੇਲੇ ਭੀ ਹੈ । ਨਾਨਕ-ਹੇ ਨਾਨਕ ! ਹੋਸੀ-ਹੋਵੇਗਾ, ਰਹੇਗਾ । ੧ ।
ਅਰਥ :- ਹੇ ਨਾਨਕ ! ਅਕਾਲ ਪੁਰਖ ਮੁੱਢ ਤੋਂ ਹੋਂਦ ਵਾਲਾ ਹੈ, ਜੁਗਾਂ ਦੇ ਮੁੱਢ ਤੋਂ ਮੌਜੂਦ ਹੈ । ਇਸ ਵੇਲੇ ਭੀ
ਮੌਜੂਦ ਹੈ ਤੇ ਅਗਾਂਹ ਨੂੰ ਭੀ ਹੋਂਦ ਵਾਲਾ ਰਹੇਗਾ । ੧ ।
ਨੋਟ-ਇਹ ਸ਼ਲੋਕ ਮੰਗਲਾਚਰਨ ਵਜੋਂ ਹੈ । ਇਸ ਵਿਚ ਗੁਰੂ ਨਾਨਕ ਦੇਵ ਜੀ ਨੇ ਆਪਣੇ ਇਸ਼ਟ ਦਾ ਸਰੂਪ
ਬਿਆਨ ਕੀਤਾ ਹੈ, ਜਿਸ ਦਾ ਜਪ ਸਿਮਰਨ ਕਰਨ ਦਾ ਉਪਦੇਸ਼ ਇਸ ਸਾਰੀ ਬਾਣੀ 'ਜਪੁ' ਵਿਚ ਕੀਤਾ ਗਿਆ
ਹੈ ।
ਇਸ ਤੋਂ ਅਗਾਂਹ ਬਾਣੀ 'ਜਪੁ' ਦਾ ਮਜ਼ਮੂਨ ਸ਼ੁਰੂ ਹੁੰਦਾ ਹੈ ।

### Sant Teja Singh (English translation & commentary)

**Roman transliteration (Sant Teja Singh's own):**

Ek onkar satnam karta purakh nirbho nirvair Akal murat ajooni saibhang gurprasad.
॥ Jap ॥
Ad sach jugad sach hai bhee sach Nanak hosi bhee sach.

**Translation & commentary:**

There is but One all embracing and all-powerful Divinity, Who manifests Himself first in the shape of the sacred word, and then through the whole created Universe.

He is the One-in-All and the All-in-One. He is the Eternal Reality, and His name Satnam is also Eternal. He is the Creator, and has the power of independent self-creation. He permeates the whole creation. He is the only male element in the Universe and all else is female. He is above all fear, and is free from all thoughts of enmity. He is immortal, free from birth and rebirth, and can be realized in every created thing.

He is self-existent. The whole universe is dependent for its existence on Him but he is self-existent; the Generator of all, without any one to generate Him.

He can be realized through the grace of the Guru: - The Teacher who is God conscious, and sees Him both within and without.

Note: - All the ten Gurus blessed men and women with the gift of the Divine name. Those who realized the Divine within were called Gurmukhs and were given the right of showing the path of realization to seekers after Truth; through meditation on the Divine name.

Nanak begs the dust of the feet of that Gurmukh who himself meditates on the Divine name, and shows other the path of so doing.

Guru Gobind Singh says, “He who has realized the Divine within is the True Khalsa. He is at-one with God and myself.”

Then O Sikh, the searcher after Truth; meditate on His name, who was real and self-existent before all creation, who was real through all ages (Yugas); who is real and true now, and shall be true and self-existent for all times to come.
