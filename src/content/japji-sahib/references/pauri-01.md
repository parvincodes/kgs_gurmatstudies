### Jarnail Singh — "Understanding Jap" (Sikhspectrum.com) — commentary on Pauri 1

This essay reads "Aad sach jugaad sach. Hai bhee sach Nanak hosee bhee sach" (the couplet just before Pauri 1) as Guru Nanak's own theory of Truth, then surveys Western philosophical theories of truth as a contrast — Correspondence (Plato, Aristotle, Russell), Coherence (Hegel, Spinoza, Bradley), Constructivist (Vico, Marx), Consensus (Habermas, Rescher), and Pragmatic (Peirce, James, Dewey) — arguing that because Truth and God are synonymous in Gurbani, Truth instead shares God's own attributes as laid out in the Mool Mantar: one, everlasting, impartial, fearless, beyond time. Key points on the pauri itself, attributed to Jarnail Singh's essay:

- Reads the first four lines (ritual purity/soch, willful silence/chup, satiating hunger, accumulating wisdom) as recipes the *mind itself* offers for reaching Truth, each of which actually thickens the "cocoon of falsehood" around a person because each keeps the mind in the driver's seat.
- Reads the fifth line — "Kiv sachiara hoiyai kiv kurai tutai pal" — as the essay's central question: how does one become a *Sachiara* (a truth-lit person) and break through the wall of falsehood?
- Reads the sixth line — "Hukam rajai chalana... Nanak likhya nal" — as Guru Nanak's answer: submitting the mind to Hukam, as taught by the Guru, is the only way; this is what separates a *Gurmukh* (Guru-led) from a *Manmukh* (mind-led).
- Cites a run of Gurbani verses on Manmukh vs. Gurmukh (e.g. p.87, p.116, p.314, p.419, p.511, p.552, p.1024) to argue that following the mind's dictates always piles up more falsehood, regardless of which recipe is used.
- Draws on literary references — Eugene O'Neill's *The Iceman Cometh* and T.S. Eliot's *Murder in the Cathedral* — to argue self-deception is pervasive across all walks of life, not just for people conventionally seen as "bad."
- Closes by connecting this pauri's demand — that the mind "die" to its own dictates — to Vaisakhi 1699, arguing (against scholars who treat the founding of the Khalsa as an unrelated event) that Guru Gobind Singh's Khalsa initiation institutionalized exactly this demand from Guru Nanak's first stanza.

### Sant Teja Singh (English translation & commentary)

**Roman transliteration (Sant Teja Singh's own):**

Sochai soch na howee je sochi lakhwar.
Chupai chup na howee je lai rahan livetar.
Bhukhian bhukh na utree je banna puria bhar.
Sehas sianpa lakh hoeh ta ek na chalay nal.
Kiv sachiara hoiyai kiv kurai tutai pal.
Hukam rajaee chalana Nanak likhya nal.

**Translation & commentary:**

In this Pauri Sat-Guru Nanak enumerates the various paths that were practiced by persons for attaining at-one-ment and peace of mind; and shows their inefficacy – By mere thinking and intellectual reasoning true wisdom (the realization of the Divine within) cannot be attained, though one goes on thinking millions of times.

By merely keeping one’s mouth shut and remaining tongue tied; one cannot restrain the mind from wandering in all directions, although one may maintain superficial silence for a very long time, like the heron or the cat watching for its prey.

By mere fasting, one can not overcome the desire of indulging in the ephemeral pleasures of the taste and tongue; though moved by this fasting the world may bring to him all the rich dainties of the whole created universe.

Thousands of intellectual flights cannot take one to the Realm of Truth (True wisdom of the realization of the Divine within).

Q. Then how shall one be at-one with truth and how shall the veil of ignorance (selfishness, worship of flesh and blood) be removed?

Ans. O searcher after Truth intune thyself with the Eternal Law which controls the whole universe, ever since its creation, and which is embedded in the very depths of the human soul.

Note: - However much depraved a person may be, the still voice within warns him at every wrong step; but his confirmed habits of wrong actions carry him away.

In the human stage of evolution every one gets the power of judging the right from the wrong. This power is engrained in every human soul. This innate power coupled with righteous actions and the company of God conscious persons who show the path of Divine Realization through meditation on the Divine name, intunes one with the infinite. One becomes at-one with the Divine Law. He submits to the Divine will under all circumstances and gets freedom from egoism. The sense of mine and thine, which forms the dark curtain of ignorance and is a bar to self-realization, is eradicated and a spiritual link established with all mankind and the created universe.

Jap Ji Sahib is the essence of the teachings of Guru Granth Sahib, the holy scriptures of the Sikhs. It deals with the main ideals which should guide a Gursikh, “A searcher after Truth”; under the directions of the Satguru.

How can we realize the truth?

This Pauri at first deals with the methods and practices generally in vogue but which do not lead to realization and peace. In the end Satguru Nanak gives his own method. Be at-one-with the Divine Law, innate in the human mind.
